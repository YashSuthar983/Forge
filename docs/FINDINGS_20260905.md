## 2. The core diagnosis

Iteration counts are **fine**. Per-iteration cost scales **wrongly**.

| rung | m | SOR iters | HiGHS iters | iter ratio | **SOR µs/iter** | **HiGHS µs/iter** |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| XL | 5 208 | 8 416 | 5 343 | 1.57× | 90 | 35.2 |
| XXL | 13 776 | 22 251 | 13 921 | 1.60× | 194 | 32.3 |
| HUGE | 34 272 | 58 012+ | 35 403 | ~1.6× | ~491 | 40.0 |

> **HiGHS's per-iteration cost is flat in m. SOR's is linear in m.**

Average FTRAN support is **constant** across the ladder (192 → 206 of up to 34 272 slots), so
a correct hypersparse implementation must be flat too. This is the whole industrial gap, and
it is why the ratio *grows* with scale rather than staying put.

## 3. The O(m) terms, localized

Instrumented `lu.cpp` directly (`perf` blocked by `perf_event_paranoid=4`), measured, reverted.

### 3.1 The RHS permute — `lu.cpp:649-659`, in **both** `ftran_impl` and `btran_impl`

```cpp
for (std::size_t k = 0; k < n; ++k) {
    const f64 value = b[sz(piv_row_[k])];   // random-access gather over all m
    work_[k] = value; ...
}
```

| rung | m | permute calls | total | **µs/call** |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| XL | 5 208 | 19 787 | 149.4 ms | 7.55 |
| XXL | 13 776 | 52 111 | 1 019.5 ms | 19.56 |
| HUGE | 34 272 | 135 912 | 6 560.3 ms | **48.3** |

Predicting HUGE from XXL by pure linearity: 19.56 × (34272/13776) = **48.7 µs**, measured
**48.3 µs** — the `c·m` model holds to 1%, with c ≈ 1.41 ns/element (a cache-missing gather).

**BTRAN is the egregious case.** Its input is `rho` with `rho[leave] = 1.0` and zeros
elsewhere (`dual_simplex.cpp:1184, 1270`) — a **single nonzero**. At HUGE that loop touches
34 272 elements to move one value, 50 122 times per solve.

**This is the item to fix.** It is also already scoped in the header — see §4.

### 3.2 `collect_seed()` — `lu.cpp:663-676` — **I was wrong about this one**

I claimed it was pure redundancy worth −12%, since `reach_` already contains the nonzeros.
I built it, ctest 22/22, iteration counts bit-identical — and it was **slower**:

| | baseline | prototype |
|---|---:|---:|
| schedule XL | ~750 ms | 802 ms |
| schedule XXL | ~4 300 ms | 4 590 ms |
| dfl001 | ~30–33 s | 37.4 s |

**Why:** the 509 ms measured at XXL is a *sequential* scan at ~0.3 ns/element — cache-friendly
and ~5× cheaper per element than the random-access permute. Replacing it with copy +

in flight when the concurrent writer broke the build, so it remains untested.

**Retract the −12%.** The O(m) *time* is real; `collect_seed` is the wrong end to attack.

## 4. The biggest win is already specified in your header — and never implemented

`lu.hpp` declares:

```cpp
bool ftran_seeded_with_support(std::vector<f64>& b,
                               const std::vector<Index>& seed_rows,
                               std::vector<Index>& support) const;

```

with an accurate doc comment ("skips the O(n) permutation/seed scan; the sparse solves then
touch only the reach"). Measured across every `.cpp` in the tree:

- **definitions: 0**
- **callers: 0**

This is exactly the §3.1 fix. The design is agreed; it just needs building. The callers
already know their supports: BTRAN's is `{leave}`, FTRAN's is column `q`'s nonzeros (~2.0 on
this family). The theorem being violated is 37 years old:

> **Gilbert & Peierls (1988)** — a sparse triangular solve must cost O(flops), never O(n).
> SOR implements their symbolic phase correctly (reach-set DFS) and then defeats it with an
> O(m) permute at the boundary.

- **Block B** — dual engine (4 instances, ~33 s, 49%; `dfl001` alone is 30 s)

**Dual phase 1 aborts at iteration 0–130, not "stalls late."** Instrumented the Dual→primal
fallback: pilot and d2q06c fail at iteration **0**, 25fv47 at 2, greenbea 6, greenbeb 20,
pilot87 25, 80bau3b 130, stocfor2 796 — all "unblocked improving column at a primal-infeasible

**Presolve is currently net-negative and must not be built first.** Running SOR on
HiGHS-presolved models:

| instance | original | presolved | engine change | outcome |
|---|---:|---:|---|---|
| stocfor2 | 341 ms | 95 ms | primal → primal | 3.6× faster |
| bnl2 | 172 ms | 93 ms | dual → dual | 1.8× faster |
| degen3 | 698 ms | 1 143 ms | **dual → primal** | 1.6× slower |

**Presolve strength gap (Netlib only):** SOR removes 0–10% of rows where HiGHS removes 7–60%

## 6. Benchmark structure — two hypotheses I killed

I looked for exploitable special structure in the ladder. **Both leads were false**, and both
failures came from my parser counting the objective row.

1. **Network flow?** Coefficients looked like all ±1 with 2-nonzero columns — which would mean
   a node–arc incidence matrix, total unimodularity, and network simplex. **False:** 0.0% of
   columns are ±1 pairs.
2. **Dense rows?** A row of degree 10 080 (= n) appeared. **Also false** — the objective again.

True structure (all three rungs): max row degree 50 (median 3), max column degree 3
(median 1), ~2.0 nnz/column. **Textbook hypersparse, nothing exotic.**

This negative is load-bearing: there is no shortcut hiding in the ladder. It is the case
general hypersparse simplex is supposed to win, and the gap is implementation.
(`blend_lp` *is* 20% ±1-pair columns, so tree-based crossover could apply there.)

## 7. Algorithm frontier (papers downloaded and read in full)

**Field consensus in 2026:** first-order on GPU (parallel, low accuracy) → crossover
(sequential, exact) → simplex clean-up. cuOpt, Gurobi, and HiGHS have all adopted it.
**GPU simplex is a documented dead end** — no parallel simplex has beaten a good sequential
one across problem classes.

**SOR's position:** has `pdhg.cpp`, `hpr.cpp`, and Vulkan — the expensive half — and **no
crossover at all** (`grep -rn crossover` = one script, three comments). So PDHG solves 8/93
and HPR 17/93 Netlib and certifies **none**.

- `pdhg.cpp` is **vanilla PDHG**: constant step, power iteration, **no restart, no primal
  weight, no averaging**. That is the 2016 algorithm; it cannot be competitive.
- `hpr.cpp` is cuPDLP-era: restart on a residual, primal weight, `restart_to(Average)`.
  Missing Halpern, reflection, and PID.

**cuPDLPx algorithm** (arXiv:2507.14051 §3, implementable as-is — put it in `hpr.cpp`):

1. **Reflected Halpern:** `z^{k+1} = (k+1)/(k+2)·[(1+γ)PDHG(z^k) − γz^k] + 1/(k+2)·z^0`
2. **Restart on fixed-point error** `r(z) = ‖z − PDHG(z)‖_P` (not duality gap / KKT), three
   triggers: sufficient decay; necessary decay + no local progress; artificial `k ≥ β·T`
3. **Constant stepsize** `η = 0.998/‖A‖₂`, deliberately replacing adaptive search
4. **PID primal weight:** `log w_{n+1} = log w_n − [K_P e_n + K_I Σe_i + K_D(e_n − e_{n−1})]`

Reported 2.5–5× on MIPLIB relaxations, 3–6.8× on Mittelmann. arXiv:2509.23903 proves
**cuPDLPx's base algorithm is a special case of HPR-LP's** — standardise on the HPR family.

**Crossover** (the missing layer): Megiddo 1991 → Andersen & Ye 1996 (start here) → Smart
Crossover 2025 (Tree-BI + perturbation) → **Concurrent Crossover** (Rothberg, arXiv:2510.24429,
Oct 2025): launch crossover from several checkpoints concurrently, take the first to finish.
**2.08× mean CPU** (29 wins / 2 losses), 1.49× GPU. Suits SOR — Auto is already a staged race.
